# Kyrgyz history: mapped course, 22 November 2026

This release collects the atlas’s earlier reviewed Kyrgyz source readings into a topic-based course on the existing timeline. It adds dated tribal geography, the late-1940s/1950 Pamir movements, the 1979 return from Pakistan, temporary residences in Turkey, pastoral geography, and two nineteenth-century battle chapters. It does not certify every historical boundary in the atlas.

## New evidence

- M. Nazif Shahrani, “The Life and Career of Haji Rahman Qul Khan”, in *Pamirs at the Crossroads*, Berlin Geographical Papers 45 (2016), pp. 57–64; Muhammet Ekber Kutlu, “Life in Ulupamir Köyü”, pp. 65–66. [Publisher PDF](https://www.geo.fu-berlin.de/geog/fachrichtungen/anthrogeog/zelf/_media_/download/Berlin_Geographical_Papers/BGP45_Pamir_Tagung_April2016.pdf), downloaded and text checked, particularly printed pp. 59–60 and 63–65. Shahrani dates detention to 1945/1946, subsequent departure only relatively, and return to 1950. The selection year 1948 represents the late-1940s interval, not a documented departure date. His loose phrase about Xinjiang becoming an autonomous region in 1950 was rejected. The record preserves a broad Taghdumbash/Qara Chuqoor regional marker, not an exact encampment. The 1982 flight to Adana is distinct from housing near Van/in Malatya and settlement at Ulupamir in 1987/1988. Kutlu’s first-person account supports housing, schooling, livelihoods and later dispersal; its population counts are historical and are not presented as current statistics.
- Ted Callahan, *The Kyrgyz of the Afghan Pamir Ride On*, AUCA Social Research Center report, subsequently *Nomadic Peoples* 11(1), 2007, pp. 39–48. [Field report](https://www.files.ethz.ch/isn/145126/report_ted_callahan_afghan_pamir_ride_on_eng.pdf). Pages 2–4 distinguish the July 1978 exodus, the October 1979 return of 54 families (about 250 people), continuing Great Pamir residence, and short seasonal movements on north- and south-facing slopes. These are separate branches and processes.
- Ch. Ch. Valikhanov, *An Outline of Dzhungaria / Очерки Джунгарии*, in *Selected Works*, ed. Nick Fielding, trans. Arch Tait (2020), printed pp. 190–191 (PDF pp. 211–212). [Edition](https://assets.ctfassets.net/owg0u79eu3xx/Jn6g6p5DMq5C6wR4tcLgC/d500bd381e516e7fae6785ab1b6c3a17/selected_works_valikhanov.pdf). Dated mid-nineteenth-century observations support Bugu, Solto, Sarybagysh (Chui only), Sayak, Cherik, Chong Bagysh, Adygine and Pamir-related group geography. No tribal headcounts, pejorative descriptions, permanent territorial ownership, or uncorroborated genealogical wing assignments are imported. The inconsistent Issyk-Kul directional wording for Sarybagysh is not mapped. The edition’s erroneous Kenesary death year is not used for the battle date.
- *Кыргызстан: улуттук энциклопедия*, vol. 4, E. Turganbaev, “Кенесары”. [Encyclopedia entry](https://encyclopedia.edu.kg/index.php/КЕНЕСАРЫ). The 1847 defeat/capture near Tokmok, Mai-Tobe and Keklik-Sengir is represented by a regional symbol; no exact battlefield perimeter or troop counts are asserted. Existing university material was cross-checked, not treated as error-free.
- Bishkek municipality, *Шаардын тарыхы*. [Municipal history](https://www.bishkek.gov.kg/ky/history). The 1862 Baitik uprising, Russian artillery intervention and autumn fall of the Pishpek fortress are used. Exact day/month wording differs across publications; the card uses the verified year and season.

## Map semantics

- Numbered pins and translucent dashed halos locate named places or broad source regions. Halos are symbols, not territorial polygons or measured pasture extents.
- Migration, campaign and economic connections remain distinct event types. Dashed connections are schematic, not surveyed trails. The Pakistan–Adana arc means air relocation, not an exact flight path or an overland crossing.
- The 1293 military campaign and the 1293 eastward relocation retain separate chapters. The 840 campaign retains Ordu-Baliq as destination. Ancient unresolved accounts do not acquire fabricated coordinates.
- Clicking a course step changes the common year and corresponding political map. Changing to an unrelated year clears its map selection. Closing the course removes its symbols and restores the normal comparison panel. Sources in the public interface are bibliographic titles without outbound links.
- No new settlement/battle content rewrites political borders. Current geopolitical geometry and earlier reviewed Chinese/Arabic/Persian accounts remain independently maintained.

## Verification

`scripts/world/kyrgyz-study-test.cjs` checks the assembled public course, references, all languages, chronological navigation, source-date intervals, return branch, flight destination, earlier records, and inclusion of new events in the general atlas. It runs as a mandatory release check. Browser checks cover actual navigation, search, language switching, numbered places, timeline changes, closing/reopening, and public deployment.
