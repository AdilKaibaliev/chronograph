# Early chronology and physical-geography review

Reviewed 16 November 2026. Changes are bounded, source-based corrections; this is not a claim that every ancient frontier is exactly known.

## Corrections applied

1. **Han / Xin, 6–9 CE.** The upstream series assigns the broad Chinese outline to Xin from 6 CE, leaving Han as a tiny remnant. Wang Mang's regency in 6–8 CE was not yet the Xin dynasty, proclaimed in 9 CE. Transfer the contemporary 6–8 CE outline to Han, retaining the source geometry, and leave the 9 CE transition to Xin. Regression controls check Chang'an and Luoyang in 5–8 and the 9 CE change. The 30 CE outline is not backdated into the Eastern Han reunification wars.
   - Rafe de Crespigny, *To Establish Peace*, chronological introduction, Australian National University: https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/3df5d9aa-26db-44c1-8372-14d21d5a4250/content
   - Metropolitan Museum of Art, *Han Dynasty (206 B.C.–220 A.D.)*: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/han-dynasty-206-b-c-220-a-d

2. **Nabataea, 25–20 BCE.** Identical source outlines precede and follow an omitted six-year interval. Nabataean kingship continued: Obodas III reigned 30–9 BCE, including the Roman expedition of Aelius Gallus. Restore the identical Nabataean outline for the gap and subtract it explicitly from the contemporary Roman polygon, which otherwise incorrectly covers Petra. Preserve Roman annexation in 106 CE. Regression checks cover every year from 27 through 19 BCE.
   - Strabo, *Geography* 16.4.21–24: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Strabo/16D%2A.html
   - Oliver D. Hoover, *Petra on the Hudson: The Nabataeans and their Coins at the ANS*, ANS Magazine 10.1 (2011), pp. 10–13; Obodas III coin dated 24/3 BCE: https://numismatics.org/wp-content/uploads/Spring2011.pdf

3. **Physical reconstruction junctions.** Retain the dated source masks. Label the northern-ice model used in the early series: CLIMBER-2/SICOPOLIS through 123000 BP, ICE-6G from 122000 BP. Show the source-junction explanation at both 123000/122000 BP and 81000/80000 BP (transition to PaleoMIST). Do not create annual ice/coast interpolation unsupported by the source.
   - Krapp et al. (2021), *A statistics-based reconstruction of high-resolution global terrestrial climate for the last 800,000 years*, section “Surface type mask”: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8397735/

4. **Why the chronology starts at 350000 BCE.** Add a three-language explanation in the earliest event panel and the static ancient-history pages. It is a rounded coverage boundary motivated by early Homo sapiens evidence, including Jebel Irhoud (315 ± 34 ka); it is not an asserted date for the beginning of humanity or the life of Adam. Each record retains its own dated range.
   - Richter et al. (2017), *The age of the hominin fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco, and the origins of the Middle Stone Age*, Nature 546:293–296, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22335

## Issues retained for further research

The inventory checks temporal gaps and large changes without automatically treating all of them as errors. A change of ruler, occupation or campaign cannot be repaired by cosmetic interpolation.

- Seleucid absence in 77–67 BCE overlaps Tigranes II's Syrian rule. Do not fill it with a Seleucid polygon. Armenian coverage needs an independently reviewed set of contemporary outlines. See Appian, *Syrian Wars* 48–49, and https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/tigran-ii/.
- Urartu's missing 7th-century coverage requires a better dated reconstruction; Rusa II's forts establish continued activity but not a surveyed outer frontier. See https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/urartu-in-iran/.
- The 458 CE Visigothic source expansion needs campaign-versus-administration review. A large one-year outline must not be silently made permanent.
- Eastern Han begins in 25 CE, while the selected territorial series resumes in 30 CE. The 25 CE Luoyang record remains available; do not equate the proclamation with immediate control of the later outline.
- Sumer and Elam have gaps around periods of political subordination and changing regional power. These require polity-specific checks, not automatic duplication of the preceding polygon.

Geometric validation detects malformed geometry and intersections. It cannot by itself establish historical correctness. Physical layers are published reconstructions with fixed temporal and spatial resolution; inland river courses and lake levels are not independently reconstructed here.
