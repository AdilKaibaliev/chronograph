# Kyrgyz geographic review — 26 November 2026

The political atlas, settlement geography and present-day administrative reference areas remain distinct. All use the existing common year. No modern country outline is projected into the medieval past, and the new layer does not confer sovereignty over a community's host region.

## Historical review

* Ancient Gekun/Jiankun references remain dated witnesses and regional points. Neither Shiji nor the other early references supplies a surveyed national border. Preserve the two geographic reference regions and do not create a 2,200-year-old Kyrgyzstan polygon.
* The source-grounded 35-vertex Sayan/Yenisei core and ninth-century military influence envelope remain separate. Existing controls for Abakan, Tuva, Orkhon, Transbaikalia and exclusions for Kashgar/Bishkek remain enforced. A map reconstruction is not an exact annually surveyed frontier.
* 1299/1300 retains continuity. From 1368 through 1702 the late-Yenisei six-point polygon is replaced by the Khakass–Minusinsk regional envelope. It does not carry Tuva or the ninth-century military reach into the late medieval/early modern profile.
* For the seventeenth century the four documented ulus regions have separate dashed envelopes: Altysar (Iyus), Isar (Yenisei left bank), Altyr (Abakan/Uybat/Askiz) and Tuba (right-bank basins). Envelopes are geographical reconstructions, not exact ownership divisions; dependent tribute regions are not assigned as direct settlement territory. They end at the existing 1703 relocation transition. Later Khakas societies are not relabelled as today's Kyrgyz diaspora.
* The suppressed six-point Tian Shan cultural polygon remains suppressed. New geographical areas follow the dated witnesses: early sixteenth-century Issyk-Kul/inner Tian Shan; Köküm Biy's Talas/Aksy; Manap Biy's northern valleys; Unkovsky's 1722 Issyk-Kul observation; Qing-era and nineteenth-century valleys. Valley boundaries are regional envelopes, not cadastral or exclusive tribal borders. Shared margins are clipped once so they cannot double-fill.
* 1919–1991 retains the existing administrative/political timeline. The new nineteenth-century regional drawings end in 1919; they do not overwrite Soviet republic boundaries. 1991–2026 keeps the independent state chronology. This review does not claim to supply the coordinates of the 2022/2025 interstate demarcation treaties.

## Modern presence and geometry

83 records combine dated historical regions, 26 modern administrative references, Pamir regions, Ulupamir village and 33 city community markers. Present-day records are a 2026 reading view, not an assertion that their communities originated in 2026. Their source observation dates remain independent of the administrative geometry's represented year (2017). No population count is extrapolated to 2026.

## Interaction and verification

The events panel contains a compact searchable disclosure; the Kyrgyz-history course links to it. Modern communities have a dedicated jump to 2026 on the same timeline and a city-marker toggle. Selecting a card fits its outline. Map dragging, keyboard activation, RU/EN/KY, reduced map clutter, territory visibility and future-mode exclusion are tested. Detailed administrative geometry is fetched once, with an abort timeout and retry control, only in the 2026 view. It is not part of initial historical geometry downloads.

Release tests retain the existing historical controls and add source/date/geometry, 325 administrative pairs, temporal UI edges, asynchronous loading and interaction checks. Licensed geometry and attribution are downloadable from the public credits page.
