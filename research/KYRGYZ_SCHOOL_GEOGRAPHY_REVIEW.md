# Kyrgyzstan textbook geography review — 23 November 2026

## Basis

The Ministry’s list published 3 August 2026 for 2026/27 (https://edu.gov.kg/posts/5164/) retains editions from earlier years; approval date is not publication date. The Russian list names the 2012 grade-7 book and 2014 grade-8 and grade-9 books. Actual source texts consulted for this map update: Chorotegin/Omurbekov, Kyrgyzstan tarykhy IX–XVIII, Kyrgyz grade 7, 2009; Omurbekov/Chorotegin, History of Kyrgyzstan XIX century, Russian grade 8, 2014; Imankulov, History of Kyrgyzstan XX–XXI, Russian grade 9, 2014. The Kyrgyz-language 2026 list names the 2013 edition (scan p. 6). The 2009 parallel edition is cited as 2009, never relabelled 2012 or 2026.

The transitional 2026 curriculum for grades 5, 7 and 8 calls for integrated Kyrgyzstan/world history and synchronous chronology. The atlas therefore keeps one year and one world map.

## Geographic coverage

- Early Tian Shan: Suyab and medieval At-Bash/Balasagun use archaeological-site documentation, distinct from Yenisei Kyrgyz identities. Early ninth–tenth-century migration is a textbook reconstruction with regional endpoints, no invented continuous road.
- Grade 7: khaganate and Chinese/Arabic witnesses remain; Karakhanids, Qara Khitai/Naiman transition, Mongols, Qaidu, Timur, Ahmad Alacha, Khalil Sultan, Muhammad Kyrgyz, Tahir, Kokum/Eshim, Manap, Dzungar campaigns, Koysary, Qing relations and Atake are connected to dated places. Existing primary-source cards take precedence where old textbook chronology differs.
- Grade 8: Kokand expansion, Jangir, Tailak, Ormon, Sherali, Alymbek, Kurmanjan, Bugu, Tokmok/Pishpek, Naryn, Baytik, Polot, Alay 1876, Andijan 1898, publishing and 1916. Separate Kemin/Kochkor/Boom/Tokmok/Karakol/Tyup/Osh cards replace an overgeneralised national pin.
- Grade 9: 1917 returns and political change, Kokand autonomy, Soviet city-by-city transitions, relief 1920, land reform, failed Mountain Region project 1922, autonomy 1924/1926, government 1927, constitution 1929, SSR 1936, repression, wartime economy, education/science, hydropower, state language, independence and conflicts through 2010. Existing atlas periods continue to 2026.
- Diaspora: existing reviewed Pamir/China/Gilgit/Adana/temporary Turkish housing/Ulupamir cards remain. No single migration is substituted for these separate branches.

This is an original geographic teaching index, not a reproduction of every textbook paragraph. Undated traditions, biographies without a secure site and unlocated battlefields are not converted into fabricated annual map facts.

## Corrections during review

610 Kyrgyz stage had inherited the unrelated Mecca event by year-only lookup. Match now requires stage title and source identity. The Kyrgyz overview stays in the Sayan–Yenisei region; Mecca retains its independent revelation event.

The speculative 1575 Pamir card anchored in Alay was removed. Atake mission and Dzungar displacement are dated to their attested event years, without invented ending years. The refugee-return range is supported through 1920 (grade 9 pp. 24–25), not arbitrarily 1921. Balasagun political transition retains independently reviewed 1134, not the older school table’s 1130. The hydropower card marks 1975 commissioning, not an unsupported 1979 completion.

## UI requirements

Local markers use the common selected year and camera. Clicking a marker/card does not select a nearby year. Moving the timeline expires events outside their interval. A visible-area list and same-year alternatives allow co-located episodes to remain accessible. A world summary and zoom-out button preserve the same year. World territory geometry is unchanged.
