# Kraków district, 1815–1846

The previous map put Kraków in the regional Kingdom of Poland polygon after
1815. The Vienna Final Act, Articles VI–IX, establishes the Free City and its
district under three-power protection and retains Austrian sovereignty over
Podgórze. The National Archives in Kraków dates the republic to 1815–1846 and
the Austrian incorporation to November 1846.

The district is digitised independently from Konstanty Żmigrodzki's *Mapa
Rzeczpospolitej Krakowskiej od roku 1815 do 1846*, Witebsk **1908**, held by
Wielkopolska Biblioteka Cyfrowa, edition 32166. It is a regional reconstruction,
not a cadastral survey. The map's printed old grid is not assumed to be WGS84.

Six named town symbols register the scan. Independent geographic references:
PTTK Małopolska, Chrzanów (1039), Trzebinia (1894), Krzeszowice (2619),
Czernichów (1602); Visit Małopolska, St Mary's Basilica in Kraków; the
Jaworzno main-square geographic description. The maximum leave-one-out
registration error is 0.951 km. The exaggerated Kraków city symbol requires
local registration at the Kazimierz river crossing: the municipal inventory
of Most Podgórski identifies its surviving abutments beneath the Bernatka
footbridge. This adjustment tapers out within 3 km and does not move the
rest of the district. `krakow-1815-reference.json` preserves the controls,
registration, provenance and generalised geometry.

The reconstructed area is approximately 1,194 km², compared with the map's
stated 1,165 km². This difference and the registration residuals are reasons
to retain the approximate regional classification, not to advertise a
metre-accurate border. The cadastral limits of individual communes have not
been reconstructed.

Checks include Kraków, Kazimierz, Chrzanów, Trzebinia, Nowa Góra, Jaworzno,
Krzeszowice and Czernichów; Podgórze, Olkusz and Oświęcim are excluded.
The same district is removed from competing Polish, Russian, Prussian and
Austrian fills during the republic and joined to Austria from 1846. It is
not made a province of any protecting power during 1815–1845. A rejected
Cliopatria polygon predominantly south of the Vistula is not used. CShapes
is used only as an independent research comparison, not copied into this
outline.

# Oregon chronology

The inherited claim caption was still displayed in 2026. The existing shape
is a Pacific Northwest regional outline, not an accurate boundary of the
state of Oregon. Retain that regional identity and US administrative
relationship; replace the obsolete caption and narrative from 1848.

The US Census Bureau's *Guide to State and Local Census Geography: Oregon*
confirms organization on 14 August 1848, reduction in 1853 and statehood on
14 February 1859. The Oregon Secretary of State's territorial records and
*Oregon Historical Quarterly*, volume 37, confirm the Washington Territory
division on 2 March 1853. Three dated events and regional phase cards are
provided in Russian, English and Kyrgyz. No old regional outline is
relabelled as the present-day state boundary.
