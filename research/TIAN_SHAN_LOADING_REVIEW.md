# Tian Shan map and delivery review — 23 November 2026

## Requested views

1356, 1832, 1910 and 1916, centered on Kyrgyzstan and the adjoining Qing/Russian frontier. The pictures contain political fills, a six-point Kyrgyz cultural polygon, contemporary reference borders, and dotted reconstruction-overlap areas. These are different types of evidence.

## Historical corrections and limits

* The invariant six-point `late-kyrgyz-tianshan` cultural polygon is not a surveyed Kyrgyz frontier. Its political-looking fill is removed. The existing source-dated places, communities, tribal observations and movements remain on the common timeline. The catalogue record stays readable.
* In 1825 Kokand's campaign reached the Chuy valley and Pishpek was established. The 1831 campaigns reached Issyk-Kul and the Inner Tian Shan; Kurtka was built in 1832. The former Qing fill over Naryn and At-Bashy was incorrect. The revision uses a regional fort-and-valley envelope, with dates and exclusions for the already mapped Russian advance. It does not assert uniform local submission, or an exact surveyed Kokand frontier.
* The textbook dates the campaigns to 1831 and the consolidation of fortifications to the following years. Issyk-Kul is therefore part of the campaign/influence envelope from 1831, not a claim that every community submitted on that date. Tailak and Atantai's resistance remains visible in 1831–1838.
* The 1356 Moghulistan reconstruction includes the Tian Shan, Ili/Almaliq and Kashgaria and excludes Samarkand. Its broad extent remains approximate. The cultural hexagon is not evidence for a separate Kyrgyz state inside it.
* In 1910 and 1916 Naryn, At-Bashy, Karakol and Murghab belong to the Russian reconstruction; Kashgar belongs to the Chinese reconstruction. The China–Russia frontier is not interchangeable with the modern Kyrgyz/Chinese national border. IBS 64, pp. 10, 14–16, distinguishes the delimited northern sector from the frontier south of Kizil Jik Dawan, which lacked a bilateral delimitation. The latter is not silently assigned to one state. Existing uncertain overlap areas are not claimed as uninhabited land.

## Movement evidence

All dated routes already reviewed in the catalogue are now visible on the ordinary map in their own years. Directional dashes and arrowheads distinguish migration (ochre), campaign (red) and communications (green). Motion can be paused and respects reduced-motion preferences. Clicking a route opens its dated source card without changing the common world year.

Added geographical detail from Omurbekov and Chorotegin, grade 8 (2014), § 2, pp. 7–12:

* Sarybagysh groups led by Atake Biy's sons moved from Chuy towards Issyk-Kul after the 1825 Kokand campaign. Only the named regions are linked.
* The 1831 northern campaign runs through Tashkent, Shymkent, Aulie-Ata and Chuy to Issyk-Kul. The other runs through Ak-Talaa, At-Bashy, Naryn and Jumgal to Kochkor. Lines connect named places and do not purport to follow every historical mountain trail.
* The 1916 Bedel–Uch-Turfan route retains its separately reviewed source. The 1917–1920 return is shown as a regional Xinjiang–Issyk-Kul direction. Imankulov, grade 9 (2014), describes the return but does not name a pass in that passage, so no pass is invented for the return.

There is still no evidence for one precisely dated, continuous Yenisei–Tian Shan migration path. Regional references are not turned into a fictitious single journey.

## Loading and verification

The previous entry page required approximately 129.5 MB of uncompressed data assignments in 162 scripts before the atlas runtime. New delivery uses lossless gzip metadata and dated geometry bundles. Only the chosen chronological band is fetched; all earlier PaleoMIST frames use the existing on-demand landscape store as well. No historical coordinates are rounded to save bytes.

Build checks restore the serialized data exactly. Additional checks compare rendered geometry on 30 critical dates against the full catalogue, test old-browser fallback, stale requests, cache reuse and network retry. A failed request keeps the current map and provides a retry button; an obsolete request cannot replace a newer selected year.

This review does not certify every border worldwide as exact. Political outlines remain historical reconstructions at the atlas scale.
