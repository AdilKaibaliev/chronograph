# Whole-catalogue review: additional confirmed interiors

The 114 unresolved raw-overlap pairs were compared with independently dated
CShapes-Europe and CShapes 2.0 records wherever applicable. War years,
unresolved territorial claims and incompatible date coverage were excluded.
495 distinct comparisons returned no geometry errors. Five dated rules in
four pairs have documentary corroboration. Only the competing fill is
trimmed where the attested neighbour already covers the same land.

The masks are intersections of the pre-existing atlas geometry and Natural
Earth country parts, not copied CShapes border coordinates. External
datasets provide attribution checks, not a claim of cadastral precision.

- China/Russia, 1895–1913: a small mainland interior near 75.3E, 40.15N is
  on the Chinese side in the dated references. International Boundary Study
  64, pp. 15–16, describes the 1881 settlement and 1882–1884 protocols,
  extending south to approximately 38°40′N. This correction is north of
  the separate unresolved Pamir claims; it does not apply the Anglo-Russian
  agreement of 1895 to China.
- Russia/Romania, 1879–1913: remove a Romanian protrusion into northern
  Bessarabia east of the Prut. The National Museum of History of Moldova
  documents Russian administration between the Prut and Dniester during
  this period. The changed patch is north of the southern Bessarabian
  lands involved in the 1856/1878 transfers.
- Prussia/Luxembourg, 1816–1829: remove the Prussian protrusion west of the
  Our. Luxembourg's cadastral administration identifies the river frontier
  and the Vianden exception. The change does not annex Luxembourg to the
  Netherlands or replace its personal-union status.
- Austria/Italy, 1862–1865: remove a duplicated lower-Po interior south of
  Austrian territory. The published 1859 Zurich treaty describes the
  Mincio–Po frontier and preserves the existing boundary from Luzzara.
  Austrian Mantua and the pre-1866 Venetian territory are retained.

# San Juan Islands

NPS's history confirms the ambiguity of the 1846 channel definition, the
1859 crisis, British marines arriving on 21 March 1860, the joint military
presence on San Juan, arbitration on 21 October 1872 and British withdrawal
on 25 November. San Juan, Orcas and Lopez are now shown with disputed
status in 1846–1871, with dated cards. US attribution begins in 1872.
The joint camps were on San Juan; the text does not place military camps
on every island. Vancouver Island remains outside the dispute.

The three island outlines use the atlas's shared Natural Earth coastlines.
Their small bays are generalised; this does not locate a precise camp
boundary. Minor islands and the full marine channel are not newly surveyed.
