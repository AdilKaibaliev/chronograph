# Northern restitution and Kirkuk campaigns — source review, 28 November 2026

## Confirmed correction

The published catalogue retained Russian ownership at all three towns throughout 1678–1771. The Moscow agreement of 1678 returned the three districts to the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. Their subsequent transfer to Russia belongs to the first partition in 1772. Three profiles are changed: the Commonwealth, the Russian Tsardom and the Russian Empire. Three localized events are added in Russian, English and Kyrgyz.

The atlas uses the treaty year. It does not assert that all three garrisons changed hands on the same day. The precise August day differs between published accounts, and implementation depended on ratification. No exact day or unsupported permanent exchange for Kyiv is added.

## Cartographic method and limits

`source-review-145-geometry.json` preserves the reference feature identity, three ground-control points and the digitized trace. The northern regional shape starts from Bennett et al., *Cliopatria* v0.2.1 (CC BY 4.0), the Commonwealth feature covering 1687, spatially limited to 27.7–31.3° E / 55.45–56.6° N. Its town inclusions were checked against sheet 3. Its own chronology is **not** used to date the restitution.

Cliopatria omits Velizh in that frame. The eastern extension is therefore traced independently from sheet 8 and fitted locally using Velizh, Usvyaty and Surazh. A small adjustment places the Velizh marker inside the boundary rather than on the engraved town symbol. This is a generalized regional reconstruction from an eighteenth-century map, not a surveyed county boundary or a claim of metre-level accuracy. The modern Belarus/Russia border is not substituted. The historic sheets themselves are not bundled with the public website.

The same polygon is removed from Russia and merged into the Commonwealth, avoiding an overlap, a gap, or three disconnected city disks. The rule stops at 1772 and leaves the existing partition transition intact. Smolensk, Velikiye Luki and Opochka are independent exclusion controls. Earlier war years and the different 1634/1654/1655 transitions are not inferred from the 1772 sheet.

## Kirkuk campaign correction

Küpeli, *History Studies* (2010), pp. 235–237, documents the intervening Ottoman recovery in 1625 and subsequent Safavid recapture following the 1626 retreat. The existing regional envelope is corrected for 1625 only; two events are added. This supersedes review 144's uninterrupted 1625–1629 assignment. Five profiles and five new events are affected in this complete release.

Heper and Öntuğ (2022), pp. 63–64, additionally cite garrison orders in BOA Mühimme Defteri 941.4, items 25, 41 and 64. Source: https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/2336528 . Küpeli's publisher PDF is indexed but returned 403; the author-posted full text was read at https://www.academia.edu/31642212/ . No exact recapture day or broader Iraqi territorial gain is inferred.

## Verification

The new test is first run against the previous publication and fails at Commonwealth ownership of Nevel in 1678. It checks all 94 years through 1771, both Russian profiles, three nearby Russian controls, Vitebsk, the years on either side of both transfers, and all three localized events. The full release gate and public deployment must pass before publication is reported complete. A separate catalogue comparison checks that unrelated profiles and all pre-existing events are unchanged. Technical checks do not certify all historical boundaries in the atlas.
