# Eastern South-West Africa boundary, 1890–1945

The displayed territorial and imperial outlines followed 20E continuously
from 28S to 18S. The point 20.5E, 20S was missing in 1890, 1900, 1914 and 1915.
Article III(2) of the Anglo-German Agreement of 1 July 1890 instead turns east
on 22S to 21E, then follows 21E north to 18S. The omitted strip is restored
from 1890 through 1945, and on the German imperial outline until 1915.
The existing 1946 outline already includes it. Pre-1890 geometry is unchanged.

Evidence: the 1890 treaty text reproduced by the International Court of
Justice in *Kasikili/Sedudu Island (Botswana/Namibia)*, judgment of 13 December
1999; UN *Summaries of Judgments, Advisory Opinions and Orders, 1997–2002*.
Office of the Geographer, *International Boundary Study 120* (1972), pages
3–4, confirms the 1890 agreement and its continuation north along 21E to 18S.
IBS 125, pages 3–5, describes the southern 20E segment and its demarcation.

This correction concerns the treaty-defined inland segment only. It does not
certify the full Namibian profile, reconstruct Caprivi's changing demarcation,
settle the Kunene or Walvis Bay disputes, or equate colonial legal claims with
uniform effective control. The later occupation and mandate remain separate
territorial layers; the strip is not made into a detached British or South
African province. Existing political relationships and cultural layers remain
unchanged. No contemporary border dataset was copied.

Validation: every year 1890–1945 must include 20.5E,20S, exclude points east
of 21E and south of the 22S turn, and retain the dated imperial relationship.
Check 1889/1890 and 1945/1946, and neighbouring political fills.
