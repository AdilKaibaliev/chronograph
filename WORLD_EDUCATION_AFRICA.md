# Africa educational additions — 24 September 2026

Local module: `scripts/world/education-africa.cjs`. Apply once after the existing territorial catalogue. This module adds three profiles, six dated area records, three event records, nine sources, and short teaching fields for the new profiles plus Mali, Kilwa and Mapungubwe. All visitor text is in Russian, English and Kyrgyz; URLs remain editorial metadata.

## Editorial choices

- **Benin, approximately 1200 onward:** a distinct Edo royal centre. Its outline is only an independently drawn vicinity of Benin City. It is neither the modern Republic of Benin nor the much larger later kingdom. The oba origin story is explicitly a dynastic tradition; no exact accession year for Eweka is fabricated.
- **Gao, approximately 700 onward:** three stages distinguish early settlement and craft exchange, a royal core around the tenth century, and the later urban centre documented by inscriptions. After 1100 the geometry is a settlement area; the article does not justify presenting a continuously independent territorial empire through 1299. The tenth-century royal residence identification is an archaeological interpretation and is described that way.
- **Gedi, approximately 900 onward:** early Swahili settlement and thirteenth-century maritime exchange. The small outline locates the archaeological site and vicinity. A second colour frame marks the later teaching phase, without inventing a land conquest or transferring the surviving fifteenth-century town walls to the thirteenth century.
- **Sosso and Mali:** the approximately 1235 victory and the role of the Manden alliance are added to chronology and Mali teaching text. The marker explicitly represents Manden, not an excavated battlefield. A separate Sosso territorial polygon has deliberately not been manufactured: the selected sources do not establish a defensible capital location and boundary. Modern Nyagassola, where the Sosso-Bala is preserved, is not used as the ancient capital.
- Mali's teaching text dates this turning point explicitly to approximately 1235 within the broader 1230s phase. The existing `mali-rise` event covers state formation around 1230; `sosso-defeat` is the distinct military turning point, not a duplicate event.
- **Kilwa and Mapungubwe:** teaching fields connect institutions with Indian Ocean commerce and social organisation. Neither is presented as a passive recipient of Eurasian history.

Years 700, 900, 1100 and 1200 are rounded editorial phase divisions. End 1300 means the local release stops at 1299, not that the society disappeared then. No political expansion is inferred merely from a larger archaeological sample.

## Sources verified in this research pass

1. Caleb Folorunso et al., **MOWAA Archaeology Project: enhancing understanding of Benin City’s historic urban development and heritage through pre-construction archaeology**, *Antiquity* 100 (409), 2026, e11. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2025.10189). Published online in 2025; issue year 2026. Supports the approximate kingdom chronology and thirteenth/fourteenth-century archaeological layers.
2. Yemisi Shyllon Museum of Art, Pan-Atlantic University, **11 Facts You Need to Know About Benin Art**. [Museum publication](https://artsandculture.google.com/incognito/story/iAXxMDvz38PV8Q). Used for the Edo identity and explicitly traditional account of the oba dynasty, not to establish precise thirteenth-century boundaries.
3. Mamadou Cissé et al., **Excavations at Gao Saney: New Evidence for Settlement Growth, Trade, and Interaction on the Niger Bend in the First Millennium CE**, *Journal of African Archaeology* 11 (1), 2013, 9–37. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3213/2191-5784-10233). Full text was checked through the author-uploaded research publication. Supports early settlement, workshops and imported finds.
4. Shoichiro Takezawa and Mamadou Cissé, **Discovery of the earliest royal palace in Gao and its implications for the history of West Africa**, *Cahiers d’études africaines* 208, 2012, 813–844. [Article](https://journals.openedition.org/etudesafricaines/17167). Do not turn its contested reconstructions of ethnic/dynastic replacement into established fact. The module uses the buildings, written descriptions and inscriptions.
5. Soichiro Takezawa and Mamadou Cissé, **The First Discovery of the Royal Palace of “Medieval” West Africa**, *Journal of African Studies* 73, 2008, 31–46. [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/africa1964/2008/73/2008_73_31/_article). Supports the royal-centre event; its approximate date is preserved.
6. UNESCO, **The Historic Town and Archaeological Site of Gedi**. [World Heritage record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1720). Supports occupation from the tenth century and its Indian Ocean context. Later surviving architectural features were not indiscriminately backdated.
7. V. V. Matveiev, **The development of Swahili civilization**, in D. T. Niane (ed.), *General History of Africa IV*, chapter 18. [UNESCO chapter](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/in/rest/annotationSVC/DownloadWatermarkedAttachment/attach_import_e8290e53-1c69-4eee-83ac-fe9540a692fb?_=184287engo.pdf&from=479&to=504). Used for Gedi's medieval imported ceramics and maritime exchanges.
8. UNESCO, **Cultural space of Sosso-Bala**. [Heritage record](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/cultural-space-of-sosso-bala-00009). Supports Sumaoro Kanté and preservation of the Sunjata cycle by griots.
9. Ann Kordas et al., **World History, Volume 1: to 1500**, section 15.2, *Medieval Sub-Saharan Africa*, OpenStax / Rice University, 2023. [Textbook](https://openstax.org/books/world-history-volume-1/pages/15-2-medieval-sub-saharan-africa). Supports the Sosso–Manden conflict and conventional 1235 turning point.
10. Existing source revisited: UNESCO, **Ruins of Kilwa Kisiwani and Ruins of Songo Mnara**. [World Heritage record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/144). Used for the Kilwa teaching fields.
11. Existing source revisited: UNESCO, **Mapungubwe Cultural Landscape**. [World Heritage record](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1099). Used for the Mapungubwe teaching fields.

These additions improve selected gaps. They do not claim an exhaustive inventory of African societies between 610 and 1299; the wider central African, Horn of Africa and Sahel sequences need further research before a later comprehensive release.
