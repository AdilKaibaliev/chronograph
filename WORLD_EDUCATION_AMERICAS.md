# Americas educational expansion — 24 September 2026

Local content module: `scripts/world/education-americas.cjs`. It appends five profiles, eight phases with government/economy/society summaries, eight territorial frames, six chronological records, and nine source records. All visitor text is provided in Russian, English and Kyrgyz. No publication or deployment was performed.

## Coverage and editorial decisions

| Profile | Mapped period | Representation and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Xochicalco | c. 650–900; c. 900–1000 | A small political core during the florescence, followed by a smaller settlement vicinity during the final occupation. UNESCO's conventional 650–900 peak is distinguished from archaeological chronologies permitting occupation into the tenth century. No named conqueror or precisely dated war is invented. |
| El Tajín | c. 800–1200 | A local urban/political core in northern Veracruz. Monumental compounds, ballcourts and coastal/highland connections illustrate urban society. This represents the well-documented peak, not the entire occupation sequence. |
| Cholula | 610–900; c. 900–1299 | The urban region persists through the end of the Classic period into the Postclassic. The two frames keep the same geographic core: a different cultural phase is not automatically territorial expansion. A c.1150 record introduces Silvia/Diana painted pottery, not a spurious political conquest. |
| Lucre / Chokepukio | c. 1000–1200; c.1200–1299 | The Lucre Basin near Cusco, with local political and ceremonial continuity after Wari. A settlement-region category avoids portraying the later Inca Empire. Around1200 concerns monumental halls documented in the Met chronology; it is not a precise state-foundation date. |
| Wanka / Upper Mantaro | c.1000–1299 | A cultural/settlement region in the Upper Mantaro and Yanamarca valleys. Archaeological Wanka I chronology, commonly1000–1350, is used instead of treating the Met's broad1250 chronology as an exact beginning. Fortified Wanka II polities chiefly belong after the current date range. No single Wanka sovereign border is asserted. |

All outlines are independently drawn, approximate regional locators. They are neither excavated city-wall plans nor recovered sovereign frontiers. The regional outlines should not be used for area/population calculations. Phase endpoints at1300 are the atlas's current cutoff, not the disappearance of Cholula, Lucre or Wanka societies. Original Maya wars and coastal Andean states remain unchanged.

## Research sources

- UNESCO, *Archaeological Monuments Zone of Xochicalco*: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/939/ — conventional650–900 florescence, hilltop settlement, political/commercial role and civic architecture.
- INAH, *Zona Arqueológica Xochicalco*: https://www.inah.gob.mx/zonas/zona-arqueologica-de-xochicalco — wider occupation chronology,650–900 principal phase, regional influences. Its discussion notes disagreement over a purely military interpretation of the hilltop position.
- Norberto González et al., *La cronología de Xochicalco*, **Arqueología37** (2008),122–139: https://revistas.inah.gob.mx/index.php/arqueologia/article/view/3687/0 — alternative archaeological dating650/700–900/1000. Search-indexed institutional article/PDF was readable; direct fetch timed out. The final century is presented as declining occupation, not precise continuous sovereignty.
- UNESCO, *El Tajin, Pre-Hispanic City*: https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/631/ —800–1200 peak, urban organisation, architectural evidence. **Excluded** its imprecise sentence associating the post1200 abandonment directly with Aztec imperial rule; that would be anachronistic within this atlas's period.
- INAH, *Zona Arqueológica El Tajín y museo de sitio*: https://inah.gob.mx/zonas/153-zona-arqueologica-el-tajin-y-museo-de-sitio — geography between the Cazones/Tecolutla basins, cultural reach and broader occupation. No exact ethnic identity is asserted for the medieval city.
- INAH, *Zona Arqueológica Cholula*: https://www.inah.gob.mx/zonas/zona-arqueologica-de-cholula and *Cholula*, Lugares INAH: https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/node/4336 — long urban sequence, trade location, valley agriculture, old pyramid complex versus later ceremonial centres. No retrospective Aztec frontier is used before1300.
- INAH, *La iconografía e iconología relacionada con el sol en los polícromos Silvia y Diana de Cholula*, **Arqueología37** (2008),140–154: https://www.revistas.inah.gob.mx/index.php/arqueologia/article/view/3688 — Tecama phase1150–1350 and solar imagery. Direct fetch timed out; institutional search-indexed article text provides the dating. The new event uses approximate1150.
- Gordon McEwan, Arminda Gibaja and Melissa Chatfield, *Arquitectura monumental en el Cuzco del Periodo Intermedio Tardío*, **Boletín de Arqueología PUCP9** (2005): https://repositorio.pucp.edu.pe/items/47ec0f90-daf9-42c1-884f-f9e88557d758 — excavation-based local polity, niched halls, feasting, elite participation and waterworks; ancestor-veneration interpretation remains attributed to researchers.
- José Alejandro Cruz Muñiz and Richard Trujillo Oquendo, *Caracterización de cerámica Lucre del sitio arqueológico de Choquepujio, temporadas2002–2003: forma, decoración y tecnología*, UNSAAC (2023): https://repositorio.unsaac.edu.pe/handle/20.500.12918/8006 — local Late Intermediate ceramic production.
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art, *Central and Southern Andes,1000–1400 A.D.*: https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/07/sac.html — c.1200 Chokepukio halls; existing source ID `andes` reused. The broad1250 Wanka entry is superseded for initial chronology by archaeological Wanka I research, without importing the later largest fortified sites into1000.
- Terence N. D'Altroy, *Provincial Power in the Inka Empire*, chapter4, *Wanka Society before the Inkas* (1992), indexed by Yale HRAF: https://ehrafarchaeology.yale.edu/traditions/se70/documents/016 — Mantaro/Yanamarca geography and distinction between Wanka I and Wanka II.
- UCLA Institute of Archaeology and Getty Conservation Institute, *Archaeometry of Pre-Columbian Sites and Artifacts* (1994): https://www.getty.edu/publications/resources/virtuallibrary/0892362499.pdf — Wanka I1000–1350, Wanka II1350–1460, household patio groups and ceramic evidence.
