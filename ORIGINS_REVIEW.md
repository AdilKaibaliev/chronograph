# Origins extension review — 15 November 2026

This release extends the existing single historical timeline, rather than opening a separate ancient atlas. The working lower bound is 350,000 BCE. It includes the rounded uncertainty window around the published Jebel Irhoud age of 315 ± 34 ka; it is not a claim that humanity began in that calendar year. Earlier human history exists outside this selection.

The continuous scale has fixed piecewise intervals, labelled as variable scale. Every historical integer year round-trips through its position; there is no year zero. Exact-year input, one-year keyboard movement and buttons remain available. Playback stops at dated changes. Political geometry and the earlier 610–2026 catalogues are unchanged.

## Evidence and date handling

Sixteen prehistoric records locate dated finds, settlements, cultivation evidence and the changing Bering land connection. Two reading sequences open the corresponding date and regional reference on the same map. They are not drawn as a single proven migration path. Earliest windows are rounded; BP ages use the conventional 1950 reference where explicitly stated. A find's dating window is not a period of continuous population occupancy. A regional marker is not an exact excavation coordinate or a territorial boundary.

Research bibliography and consulted public source URLs are preserved in `scripts/ancient/prehistory.cjs`. Sources include Richter/Hublin (Jebel Irhoud), Freidline (Tam Pà Ling), Clarkson (Madjedbebe), Mylopotamitaki (Ranis), Fu (Ust’-Ishim), Pigati/USGS (White Sands), Dillehay (Huaca Prieta), Snir (Ohalo), Bar-Yosef (Natufian culture), Tübingen/TISARP (Chogha Golan), UNESCO (Göbekli Tepe, Jericho), Zuo (rice), Piperno (maize) and Farmer/Princeton (Bering Strait). Publisher abstracts or institutional copies were used when complete articles were unavailable. No claim that the entire literature was exhaustively reviewed is made.

Madjedbebe's early date is identified as a published estimate, not a universally settled first-arrival date. Ohalo's trial cultivation is attributed to its authors, and not described as established domesticated wheat/barley. Chogha Golan documents a sequence from wild plants to domesticated emmer. Huaca Prieta describes intermittent occupations beneath a later mound. Monte Verde was not added: a current dating dispute needs separate review. The map retains modern reference coastlines; prehistoric shorelines and inland waters have not been globally reconstructed.

## Prophets and peoples

The trilingual source index contains 25 named figures and five peoples, with exact surah/verse or hadith references. These are concise original summaries, not purported verbatim quotations. `scripts/ancient/scriptural.cjs` preserves references and source URLs; visitor source labels remain plain text.

No exact birth year, flood date, dynasty identification or migration line is fabricated. Nuh's 950 years are a duration in Qur’an 29:14, not a BCE date. Adam, Idris and other unlocated accounts receive no invented coordinates. Al-Ahqaf and Madyan retain names without guessed exact points. The Jesus/Isa first-century era is distinguished as historical chronology (Tacitus, Annals 15.44), not a year stated in the Qur’an. Muhammad's existing chronology opens at 610; the new index does not redate his biography.

Four geographic references are available: Mecca (Qur’an 2:127 and 48:24), Egypt as a region (12:99 and 10:87), al-Hijr (15:80, Bukhari 3378, UNESCO) and Marib as the archaeological centre of Saba (27:22, 34:15, UNESCO). Marib is not presented as Sulayman's birthplace. The al-Hijr monumental tombs are Nabataean, and their dates are not assigned to Salih. Textual references are shown with an explicit undated/source banner and cleared on changing the year or entering Future Events.

Two additional dated archaeological cards concern Hegra and the Marib monument complex. Their dates describe archaeology, not the calendar date of a Qur’anic account. No new ancient political polygons are invented from scriptural names.

## Verification

Calendar and scale tests cover 352,026 historical years and the BCE/CE seam. Data tests require three languages, referenced sources, unique prehistoric records, valid coordinates, complete reading sequences and absence of invented exact prophetic dates/routes. The regular ancient tests compare the delivered records and scriptural index with the reviewed source. Final browser and publication evidence is recorded outside the source project in `outputs/origins-*`.
