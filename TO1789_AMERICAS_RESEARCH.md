# Americas, 1601–1789: evidence and mapping decisions

Research checked 24 September 2026. This module extends the local edition only. Sources appear as bibliographic titles in the interface; research URLs remain metadata.

## Coverage

`scripts/world/to1789-americas.cjs` contributes 57 profiles with 139 dated phases and matching territorial frames, plus 27 located events. It continues all 33 American profiles active in 1600 and adds 24 profiles. The final half-open interval ends at 1790, so 1789 is included. Earlier phases, territorial frames and events are unchanged.

The layer differentiates colonial administrative cores, settlements, Indigenous political influence and continuing cultural communities. A treaty claim is not treated as continuous occupation. Outlines are schematic regional outlines, not surveyed boundaries or a complete village inventory.

Map labels use short society or polity names, independently of the narrative phase heading. Time-sensitive labels distinguish Five/Six Nations (1722), the Itza state/communities (1697), San Gabriel/Santa Fe (1610), and changing colonial rulers. This prevents phase headings such as “The federal government begins” from becoming a country label.

## Principal decisions

- **Indigenous continuity:** archaeological labels do not imply extinct populations. Purépecha, Mixtec, Maya, Andean, Sierra Nevada, Tupinambá, Xingu and other communities continue after colonial conquest. Mississippian/Coosa umbrella entries give way to the separately represented Muscogee and Cherokee political landscape; 1700 is a display transition, not a date of ethnic disappearance. Caddo, Haudenosaunee, Pueblo, Comanche and Great Lakes entries remain visible alongside colonies.
- **English Atlantic:** Jamestown starts in 1607, Plymouth in 1620, Massachusetts expansion in 1630, Charleston in 1670, Pennsylvania in 1682 and Georgia in 1733. The shapes represent selected coastal settlement districts, not western charter claims. Indigenous diplomacy, dispossession, slavery and resistance accompany the political narrative.
- **New Netherland/New York:** Dutch rule 1624–1663, English 1664–1672, Dutch restoration in 1673 and English restoration in 1674 are separate frames. New York joins the contested United States layer in 1776.
- **Québec:** settlement from 1608; English occupation 1629–1631; French return in 1632; Montréal added from 1642. Québec falls in 1759, Montréal in 1760; the former is therefore not treated as proof that all Canada was already conquered in 1759. The treaty transfer is dated 1763. The 1774 Quebec Act is described without colouring the whole Native interior as occupied.
- **Acadia:** Port-Royal is represented as a small colonial centre through destruction, restoration and English/French transfers. English capture in 1710 and recognition in 1713 are distinct. The shape is not the whole later Nova Scotia or Mi’kmaq homeland. French administration resumes in 1670 after the 1667 treaty, distinguishing agreement from local implementation.
- **Louisiana:** Biloxi from 1699, Old Mobile from 1702, relocated Mobile from 1711 and New Orleans from 1718. Fontainebleau (1762) transfers the western claim to Spain, while French administration continues temporarily; Ulloa arrives in 1766 and Spanish authority is consolidated in New Orleans in 1769. St Louis is a separate settlement from 1764 with local Spanish administration from 1770. No full Louisiana Purchase-shaped possession appears.
- **Gulf and Florida:** Mobile changes from Britain to Spain in 1780, Pensacola in 1781, and East Florida by treaty in 1783. Havana is removed from the Spanish port layer during British occupation in 1762 and restored in 1763.
- **United States:** 1776 marks declared independence and contested authority (`influence`), 1783 British recognition, and 1789 the federal government. The post-1783 fill shows eastern settlement cores and a Kentucky centre. The treaty's Mississippi boundary is explained in text: Britain could not thereby extinguish Indigenous landholding and sovereignty. California, New Mexico, Mexico and Ontario remain outside the US layer.
- **Haudenosaunee/Pueblo/Comanche:** Five Nations become Six with Tuscarora accession in 1722; wartime divisions and the 1779 devastation precede the 1784 Grand River settlement. Spanish New Mexico disappears during Pueblo autonomy, 1680–1691, while Pueblo society remains. Vargas returns in 1692 with subsequent resistance explicitly retained. Comanche–Ute negotiations in 1786 are located at Santa Fe, not at the earlier 1785 Arkansas River council.
- **Alta California:** San Diego in 1769, Monterey in 1770 and San Francisco in 1776 are separate small settlement cores. A coast-wide Spanish possession is not backdated to initial contact. Kumeyaay communities are named at San Diego.
- **Maya:** independent Itza rule persists until the fall of Nojpetén in 1697. Afterward the Itza entry continues as a cultural community and a Spanish outpost appears at the lake; conquest of the capital is not equated with occupation of every forest district.
- **Caribbean:** Jamaica changes from Spanish to English in 1655. Maroon communities receive their own areas and the 1739 treaties. Saint-Domingue is limited to western Hispaniola from Spanish recognition in 1697. The slave plantation economy and resistance are described; the later Haitian Revolution is not backdated into this interval.
- **Spanish South America:** New Granada is created in 1717, abolished in 1723 and restored in 1739. Bogotá, Cartagena, Quito and Panama change administrative grouping accordingly. Río de la Plata begins in 1776 as a viceroyalty, while its existing colonial towns are already visible before that date. Potosí and Chuquisaca join its Upper Peru/Charcas group in 1776. Lima and Cusco remain in Peru. The latitude-based subdivision is a schematic separation of central Andean cores, supplemented by actual city centres; it is not presented as a surveyed 1776 boundary.
- **Mapuche/Chile:** the 1641 Parliament of Quilín and the Biobío frontier accompany a continuing independent Mapuche zone. Spanish central Chile is a distinct northern core. Patagonia and the Chaco are not silently added to Spanish control.
- **Brazil:** coastal cores are continued; Belém is added in 1616. Dutch Salvador exists in 1624, Portuguese control returns in 1625; Dutch Pernambuco 1630–1653 returns in 1654. Inland mining is represented around Minas Gerais c.1700 and Cuiabá from 1719. The 1750 Madrid treaty changes diplomacy, not an instant fill to modern Brazilian borders. The capital moves to Rio in 1763 without erasing Salvador. The Xingu and central Amazon are not painted as continuously controlled by Portugal.
- **Palmares:** autonomous mocambos remain visible, followed by the destruction of the principal fortified centre in 1694 and Zumbi's continuing resistance until his death in 1695. The small 1694–1695 frame represents the surviving resistance, not an intact capital or the end of all Brazilian quilombos.
- **Túpac Amaru II:** the 1780 uprising is situated near Yanaoca/Tungasuca in the Cusco region. Its causes and wider continued resistance are described; colonial control is not automatically removed throughout Peru.

## Conflicting or oversimplified source wording

The US Office of the Historian overview of the 1763 settlement gives an incorrect treaty name for the separate 1762 Louisiana cession. The Spanish state archive's actual 3 November 1762 Fontainebleau record is followed instead. The 1763 European settlement and delayed local implementation remain distinct.

A short Palmares public-history page contains internally inconsistent wording about 1694/1695. The Fundação Joaquim Nabuco study and the Fundação Cultural Palmares Serra da Barriga dossier support destruction of the principal centre in 1694 and Zumbi's death in 1695; these are the dates used.

Colonial administrative overviews often show legal claims across large Indigenous territories. This module deliberately uses the documented towns and regional cores for such claims, with Indigenous societies still visible. Generic centuries and c.1700 transitions are shown as approximate rather than invented day-level chronology.

## Verification

An isolated module load with `start:1601,max:1789` passed. It was not applied a second time to an integrated catalog.

- All 33 profiles active in 1600 have a continuation beginning in 1601.
- All pre-1601 phases and territorial objects remain byte-for-byte unchanged.
- All 139 phases have matching territorial frames, complete Russian/English/Kyrgyz title, text, period and three learning fields, and valid source references.
- All representative phase centres lie inside their own area; every polygon has finite coordinates and no proper self-intersections.
- Every year from 1601 through 1789 was checked for Cusco, Mexico City, Potosí, Salvador, Recife, Havana, New York, Santa Fe, San Diego, Monterey, San Francisco, Cuiabá and St Louis. Negative checks exclude remote American places from premature US or Portuguese control.
- The 27 events have complete translations, valid sources and an active referenced profile in their event year.
