# Eurasia, 1454–1600: editorial research and implementation record

Local extension only. Implemented in `scripts/world/early-eurasia.cjs`; it does not change the renderer, build scripts or deployment. All 1453 Eurasian profiles have an explicit disposition: 36 continued with new historical phases, and Rasulid Yemen ended at 1454 and was replaced by Tahirid Yemen. There are 51 new profiles: **87 profiles, 169 dated territorial frames and 33 located events** in this module. Text, titles and teaching notes are in Russian, English and Kyrgyz.

Intervals are half-open: `1454–1601` includes the displayed year 1600. Existing phases before 1454 and their source records are preserved. The Ottoman territorial colour is `#bc815a`, shared with the Africa module's Egyptian, Syrian and North African provincial profiles. Those provinces are parts of the same empire; the regional cards are not declarations of independence.

## Principal historical decisions

### Ottoman, Iranian and Arabian lands

- Ottoman frames distinguish Serbia 1459, Morea 1460, Trebizond 1461, Bosnia 1463, Karaman 1474, eastern Anatolia 1515, central Iraq 1534 and Buda 1541. Buda is outside the Ottoman core through 1540. The aftermath of Mohács in 1526 is represented by a contested Hungarian kingdom, not immediate Ottoman annexation of Hungary. Basra is not placed under Ottoman government in 1534.
- Trebizond, Serbia, Bosnia and Morea retain their own cards until their conquests. Royal Hungary and the Transylvanian/eastern Hungarian region are separate after 1541. The Habsburg Austrian and Bohemian crowns retain their institutional character.
- Qara Qoyunlu ends at 1468. Aq Qoyunlu retains its western homeland after gaining Iran. Its 1501 loss of Tabriz is separated from the 1503 loss of central Iran, Fars and Kerman; Iraqi and Diyarbakir remnants persist to the conquests of 1507–1508. A small Diyarbakir Safavid area bridges 1507–1515 before Ottoman incorporation. The specific TDV city history distinguishes acknowledgment of Isma‘il in 1507 from the appointed governor’s entry after resistance in 1509; the 1507 frame is an influence phase with that explanation.
- The Safavid sequence is Tabriz/Azerbaijan 1501, Iranian conquests 1503, Baghdad 1508, Khorasan following Merv 1510, Chaldiran 1514, loss of Baghdad 1534, contraction in 1590, and recovery of Khorasan and the Isfahan capital in 1598. Herat is outside the early Iranian core before 1510. Temporary occupation of Tabriz in 1514 is not modelled as permanent Ottoman annexation.
- Mecca's sharifs retain local government when their acknowledged overlord changes from the Mamluk sultan to the Ottoman sultan in 1517. Tahirid Yemen replaces the Rasulids in 1454 and ends in 1517. The contested interval to 1538, Ottoman coastal domains from 1538, and northern Zaydi highlands have separate regional profiles.

### Central Asia, Kyrgyz communities and Russia

- Timurid Transoxiana and Khorasan are distinguished from the Shaybanid conquests; Herat changes hands in 1507 and Khorasan again after Merv in 1510. Babur's continued career south of these conquests is stated explicitly. The Shaybanid profile ends at 1599; a Janid Bukhara profile begins then, following the chronology used by Encyclopaedia Iranica. The wider scholarship has 1599–1601 variations for the dynastic establishment; the card describes a transition, not a precisely surveyed territorial transfer on one day.
- The Kazakh Khanate's conventional 1465–1466 beginning is approximate and derived from scholarship discussing *Tarikh-i Rashidi*. Its later steppe polygon is an influence region, not a permanently unified bureaucratic frontier. Moghulistan gives way to western Tarim/Yarkand and eastern Turfan centres in 1514.
- Yenisei and Tian Shan Kyrgyz communities remain two cultural-historical regions. Their existence is not conflated with a perpetual Kyrgyz Khaganate. No single migration arrow, exact resettlement year, modern Kyrgyz border or invented Chinese quotation is supplied. The new-period evidence is the UNESCO historical synthesis and its cited sources; earlier Chinese evidence is preserved from the preceding module, not artificially redated to the sixteenth century. A specialist edition-by-edition Chinese textual study remains a distinct research task.
- Moscow is divided at Ivan III's accession in 1462. Novgorod's independent phase ends in 1478. The new tsardom profile starts at the 1547 coronation. Kazan and Astrakhan enter its regional map only in 1552 and 1556 respectively. The Crimean card distinguishes the 1478 Ottoman relationship from direct incorporation. The Great Horde terminates in 1502; Nogai groupings and the western Siberian khanate are independent regional subjects.
- The Siberian khanate's shrinking resistance zone survives the loss of its centre in 1582. A small river-fort settlement area starts with Tobolsk in 1587; the map does not colour all Siberia as Russian. The 1582 resistance marker is a regional representative point, not a fixed new capital.

### South, East and Southeast Asia

- Delhi transitions Lodi → Mughal (1526) → Sur (1540) → Mughal restoration (1555). Malwa is added only from the 1561 campaign and remains present in all later Mughal frames. Mandu is inside that region. Gujarat enters at the completion of the 1572–1573 campaigns; Bengal enters in 1576. The early Mughal core excludes Kabul, Sind and Kashmir rather than borrowing later imperial extent. Other campaigns in the 1580s–1590s are not all separately drawn.
- Bahmanid Bidar and the independent Bijapur, Ahmadnagar and Golconda courts are distinct. Vijayanagara loses its northern capital after Talikota in 1565 but retains southern dynastic domains. The map does not equate the city's destruction with the disappearance of all its people or successor authority.
- Ming is a conservative Chinese core, not a claim over Taiwan, Korea, Tibet or Mongolia. Joseon remains a separate state during the 1592–1598 invasions. Japanese armies' presence is described as invasion rather than Korean incorporation. Japan distinguishes the Onin/Sengoku conflicts, later unifiers, and the victory at Sekigahara in 1600; Tokugawa's shogunate is explicitly dated to 1603, beyond the range.
- Champa retains a southern region after the 1471 loss; Khmer courts continue after Angkor and the 1594 fall of Longvek. Ayutthaya's 1569–1584 Toungoo dependence is shown without claiming that it ceased to exist. Toungoo's broad expansion is classified as influence over dependent courts, and contracts after 1599.
- Malacca's port transfers in 1511, while a separate Malaccan-dynastic/Johor profile preserves local political continuity. Bintan is a regional illustration of changing bases during 1511–1528, not a claim of uninterrupted royal residence there. Johor's new centre begins in 1528. The Portuguese outline remains a small port district. Spanish Cebu begins in 1565 and Manila in 1571; neither outline claims the whole Philippine archipelago.

### Europe

- Castile and Aragon remain distinct crowns after the 1479 dynastic union. Granada enters Castile only in 1492. Upper and Lower Navarre diverge in 1512; the northern crown remains present. Portugal keeps its institutions and a separate colour/profile under the shared monarch after 1580.
- France includes the religious-war and Nantes settlement phases. England retains Calais before 1558 and loses its continental ring thereafter. Scotland remains separate. The Holy Roman Empire is an influence layer of principalities and cities; it is not depicted as a unitary state. The Reformation and Augsburg settlement have dated phases.
- Poland and Lithuania remain distinct until their 1569 commonwealth profile, whose text preserves the grand duchy's separate law, treasury and army. The outline is a regional political framework and does not claim to resolve every frontier adjustment in the Muscovite wars.
- Added Austria, Bohemia, Hungary and its successors, Venice, Milan, Florence/Tuscany, Papal lands, Naples, Scotland, Denmark–Norway, Sweden, the Netherlandish provinces and the United Provinces. The 1581 abjuration is distinguished from an instant, universally recognised Dutch republic. Danish–Norwegian and Swedish histories remain separate within and after the contested Kalmar union.

## Geometry and limits

All added geometry is tagged `schematic-regional-outline` and approximate. Polity cores, influence zones, settlement districts and cultural regions are distinct kinds. These are editorially drawn regional outlines anchored to named places and historical sequences; no claim is made that the cited books supplied GIS boundary vertices. Small islands, exclaves, yearly campaigning fronts, all minor principalities, and many Ottoman or Mughal provincial conquests remain outside this bounded overview. HRE member areas and dependencies can overlap a wider influence layer intentionally.

The research does not treat an uncoloured place as empty of people or politics. A broad late-Majapahit/early-sultanate transition around 1500, the rise of inland Mataram around 1580, the establishment of Astrakhan around 1460, and the Kazakh formation date are approximate. The sixteenth-century Kyrgyz cultural regions have continuity without an invented single migration or a claim of uniformly centralized rule. Event coordinates identify the named town or regional scene, not a surveyed battlefield perimeter.

- All 37 active Eurasian profiles at 1453 are accounted for (36 continuations, one explicit termination).
- Russian, English and Kyrgyz fields and bibliography IDs exist; every new phase has a territorial frame.
- Point-in-polygon checks cover Buda before/after 1541, Kazan 1552, Astrakhan 1556, Malacca 1511, Delhi 1526/1540/1555, Mandu before/after 1561 and its retention in 1573/1576, Herat before/after 1510, and Granada before/after 1492. Diyarbakir continuity is Aq Qoyunlu through 1506, Safavid influence from 1507, and Ottoman rule from 1515.
- Independent agent audit checked all years for Ming exclusions (Taipei, Taichung, Kaohsiung, Seoul, Lhasa, Ulaanbaatar), key conquest transitions, preservation of earlier phases, self-intersections and representative phase coordinates. Reported coordinate and Mandu issues were corrected.
