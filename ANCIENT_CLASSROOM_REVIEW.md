# Ancient chronology and classroom presentation — 15 November 2026

This release expands the existing single timeline. It does not claim a complete annual reconstruction of ancient world borders or calendar dates for every prophet.

## Historical additions

China: Liangzhu, Yinxu, the Zhou court's eastward move, recognition of the three Jin successor houses, six dated Qin conquest cards, Chu–Han rivalry, and twelve capital/centre records. Qi's court retreats to Ju in 284 BCE and returns to Linzi in 279. Chu moves from Ying to Chen in 278, to Juyang in 253 and to Shouchun in 241. Shiji chapter 15 supplies the chronology; chapters 46/82 describe Qi's recovery. The Juyang map marker is a Taihe regional reference, not an identified building. Western Zhou now includes 771 BCE; Eastern Zhou starts in 770. Spring and Autumn / Warring States use the Met's 475 BCE periodisation. Dates identify events or periods of evidence, not an asserted founding year for every people.

The source-led Zhou court connector links the Feng–Hao area to Luoyang. It is a schematic connection, not an excavated road or a migration of all inhabitants.

Five early Kyrgyz source profiles are accessible directly from the ancient navigation. Their classical Chinese passages are now displayed alongside the translated account and bibliography. The approximate 201 and 49 BCE anchors have not been promoted to exact dates. Gekun/Jiankun locations remain unresolved; no ninth-century Kyrgyz territory is backdated.

## Border checks and rejected material

Cliopatria's individual Warring States outlines failed independent capital checks: Daliang was assigned to Chu, and Xinzheng, Ji and Handan also had contradictory assignments in some frames. These candidate shapes were **not published**. Documented capitals and events remain on the global map. A separate reference illustration, explicitly dated about 250 BCE, is available in the China reading guide; it is not automatically overlaid on other years.

The reference SVG is Ian Remsen's *Warring States c. 250 BC*, 6 February 2025, CC0, based on *The Cambridge History of China*, volume 1 (1986), p. 39. Original: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Warring_States_c._250_BC.svg . The SVG is preserved unchanged with a translated colour legend. An experimental georeferencing attempt was not accepted for publication.

Qin's imperial label/geometry now begins in 221 BCE using the union of the contemporary Qin and Qi frames, rather than a later maximum extent. Han in 206–203 BCE is identified separately from Western Chu; the earlier state Han 韓 is not conflated with Liu Bang's Han 漢. Capital checks cover six Qin centres in 221 BCE and Han/Chu centres through 206–203 BCE. The 609–610 continuity bridge remains unchanged.

## Prophets, people and geography

The 25 existing prophet profiles retain `date: null` unless a separately labelled historical chronology is available. No numeric age of humanity or exact mission year has been inferred from genealogies. Relations explicitly attested in the Qur'an supplement calendar dating: for example Ad after Nuh's people (7:69), Thamud after Ad (7:74), Musa with Harun (19:53), Sulayman succeeding Dawud (27:16).

New regional references from Ibn Kathir's tafsir: Yunus/Nineveh (10:98), Madyan near Ma'an on the Hijaz road (7:85), and the reported Hadramawt/al-Shihr identifications of al-Ahqaf (46:21). These circles are geographical references to named interpretations, not surveyed state boundaries, birthplaces or proof of an exact settlement. A point on the map does not date a prophetic mission. Calendar dating, audience and location are separate fields.

Chronological caution itself follows Islamic scholarship: IslamQA answer 225177, citing Ibn Kathir's *Al-Bidaya wa-n-nihaya*, 1:237–238, explains why a complete elapsed chronology cannot be derived. This does not imply that every statement in every historical compilation is authentic; each claim needs its own evidence.

## Presentation and validation

The ancient Compare tab now shows contemporary records grouped by world region. The Events panel exposes the current territorial index and shortcuts to farming, cities, kingdoms, early CE and the seventh century. China, Kyrgyz and prophetic guides have direct buttons. Timeline and internal scrolling remain in the existing viewport layout.

Checks cover all 352,026 selectable historical years (no year zero), source references, the three languages, active dates, reference-SVG safety, polygon intersections, capital controls, 609–610 continuity, mobile layout, keyboard access and production assets. Exact test outputs are in the release verification reports.

Remaining research: individual pre-Qin sovereign boundaries need a better georeferenced source; the historical-year map is a growing selection of evidence, not a complete inventory of ancient habitation. Lack of a marker never establishes that a region was uninhabited. Most ancient prophetic mission years are not recoverable from the accepted sources.
