# Americas, 1300–1453 — editorial record

Prepared 24 September 2026. Module: `scripts/world/late-americas.cjs`.
This record is for editing and review. The public source list uses publication titles without links.

## Scope

10 new profiles, 21 continued profiles, 39 new phases, 40 regional frames, 6 events and 16 source records. All new prose has Russian, English and Kyrgyz versions. Earlier content through 1299 is retained; old intervals extending past 1300 are split at the new phase boundary by the shared continuation helper.

New profiles: Tenochtitlan, Purépecha state, Yucatán kingdoms after Mayapan, Sonoran communities after the Hohokam settlement transition, Rio Grande Pueblos, Pecos, Cusco/Inca, Aymara lordships, Upper Xingu, Tairona. Starting a profile at the release boundary in 1300 does not date that society's foundation to 1300.

The extension distinguishes a sovereign polity, a sphere of influence, a settlement and a cultural region. Every outline is a schematic regional reconstruction. It is not a surveyed border or a claim of exclusive administration throughout the filled region. No numerical population or territorial-area estimates are invented.

## Chronology and mapping decisions

| Case | Evidence used | Representation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tenochtitlan | INAH Templo Mayor and Mito de la peregrinación give the conventional 1325 foundation. Barlow's INAH study discusses the 1428 Tepanec war and subsequent Basin of Mexico campaigns. | Island settlement from 1325; alliance core from 1428; a larger basin core from 1432. This is explicitly a **core**, not the full later tribute empire. No 1519 maximal extent. |
| Purépecha | José Luis Punzo Díaz's INAH account describes Tariacuri, the three related centres, and Tzintzuntzan's predominance in the first half of the fifteenth century. It attributes major outer expansion to Tzitzispandacuare from 1454. | Rounded fourteenth/fifteenth-century phases. Basin-centred influence only through 1453. Later Michoacán-wide imperial boundaries are not backdated. |
| Mayapan | INAH gives the city's end around 1450. Kennett et al. combine archaeology, historical records and environmental evidence for conflict and institutional dissolution around the mid-fifteenth century, with continuing Maya states and markets. | Stable regional capital in 1300–1400; a smaller mapped centre during the crisis; cultural region of surviving Yucatán kingdoms from approximately 1450. The 1450 boundary is approximate, not an exact destruction date. |
| Cahokia | NPS provides occupation through approximately 1400 and a florescence ending earlier. | Declining settlement during the fourteenth century, absent as an active urban centre after 1400. Mississippian societies remain on the map. |
| Moundville | University of Alabama Museums describes a change around 1350 from a populous town to a continuing ceremonial and political centre, with further decline. | Reduced settlement area from approximately 1350. No claim that all occupation or all regional people ended in 1450. |
| Caddo | University of Texas archaeology pages distinguish Middle and Late Caddo, with late local clusters after about 1400 and many independent societies. | Two phases, with multiple cultural clusters in the later frame. No unified Caddo empire. |
| Pueblo | NPS Bandelier describes large villages from around 1300 and Rio Grande Classic Period 1325–1600. NPS Pecos gives occupation from about 1350 and aggregation in the fifteenth century. | Rio Grande/Pajarito cultural region from 1300; Pecos settlement from approximately 1350. Mesa Verde and Chaco are not revived as late medieval political centres. |
| Hohokam and Paquimé | NPS Casa Grande documents building around 1350 and major settlement changes about 1450. INAH Paquimé research uses a 1200–1450 Middle period. | Pre-1450 centres end approximately; a separate Sonoran-community phase continues without asserting that a people disappeared. Paquimé is not mechanically extended past its archaeological end. |
| Lambayeque/Chimú | Peru's Ministry of Culture and Lambayeque's archaeological guide describe Túcume's succession around 1375. | Chimú expands north into Lambayeque around 1375; Túcume remains a settlement inside the larger polity rather than a second independent state. |
| Inca | Met chronology associates the imperial transition with 1438 and early power with Cusco and adjacent valleys; British Museum gives Pachacuti's conventional reign 1438–1471. | Regional Cusco core before 1438, enlarged neighbouring-valley influence thereafter. No Quito-to-Chile empire in 1453; accession date marked approximate/traditional. |
| Central Andes | Met regional accounts; Peru Ministry of Culture's Pachacamac management plan; Wanka and Lucre research already in the catalog. | Chincha, Ichma, Wanka and local settlements retain their identities. Ichma's conquest around 1470 is outside this release. Aymara shading is a multi-polity cultural region. |
| Northern Andes and Chile | Existing university, museum and national-library references; British Museum and Met Tairona collections. UNESCO dates San Agustín's archaeological abandonment around 1350. | Muisca, Tairona and El Vergel continue; San Agustín's late site phase ends approximately 1350. |
| Amazonia | Prümers et al. date Casarabe approximately 500–1400. Cristiana Barreto discusses Marajoara changes about 1400. Heckenberger et al. document Upper Xingu settlement clusters, roads and managed landscapes in the late precolonial period (their investigated development interval is roughly 750–450 years BP). | Casarabe and Marajoara phase outlines end approximately 1400; Upper Xingu communities remain. These are archaeological phase boundaries, not dates of regional depopulation. |

Geometry reused with `previous` is confined to a continuing named valley, basin, archipelago or cultural region. It preserves a previously reviewed **schematic spatial reference**; it does not assert unchanged political borders. New political transitions receive different shapes or categories. Moundville, Mayapan, Caddo, Tenochtitlan, Purépecha, Chimú and Cusco have explicit changed frames.
