# Americas, 1454–1600: research and mapping decisions

Research checked 24 September 2026. This file records editorial evidence for
`scripts/world/early-americas.cjs`; URLs are metadata for review and are not
public-facing source links. No deployment is part of this change.

## Scope and chronology

- 42 profiles have content in the new interval: 24 continuations and 18 new
  profiles. The new module adds 99 dated phases/territorial frames, 25 located
  events and 24 bibliography records. Russian, English and Kyrgyz are supplied
  for titles, descriptions, names and learning text.
- All profiles active in 1453 receive a phase beginning in 1454. The cutoff is
  the next coverage interval, not a claimed simultaneous historical foundation.
- Historical intervals are half-open, ending at 1601 to include 1600. The module
  preserves all pre-1454 phases and territories. Archaeological period endings
  are not asserted to be population extinctions.
- Sources distinguish dated events from approximate archaeological and political
  phases. All outlines are schematic regional cores. Successive outlines do not
  claim exact annual boundaries. `approx:true` is applied to regional phases;
  documentary milestone dates remain separate.

## Principal source groups

### Central Mexico and Yucatán

1. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, **Mexico, 1400–1600 A.D.** and
   **Tenochtitlan**, alongside **Golden Kingdoms: Exhibition Galleries**.
   These support a tributary alliance, independent Tlaxcala and Purépecha
   neighbours, conquest in 1521 and the viceroyalty in 1535.
   - <https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/canm.html>
   - <https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/tenochtitlan>
   - <https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2018/golden-kingdoms/exhibition-galleries>
2. Met, **Paying the Man: Ancient Tributes in Golden Kingdoms**, interpreting
   the Codex Mendoza and Codex Kingsborough. Provides documentary evidence for
   tribute and changing colonial burdens.
   - <https://www.metmuseum.org/fr/perspectives/golden-kingdoms-ancient-tributes>
3. José Luis Punzo Díaz, **Tzintzuntzan**, INAH; INAH,
   **México, grandeza y diversidad**, conquest chapter. Supports expansion from
   Michoacán, Spanish subjugation beginning in 1522 and Tangaxoan II’s death in
   1530. The post-1522 Purépecha community profile is separate from the former
   independent state.
   - <https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/node/5548>
   - <https://mexicograndezaydiversidad.inah.gob.mx/images/fasciculos/MexicoGrandezaDiversidad_F-09.pdf>
4. INAH, **Cholula** and **1519–1521: alianzas, confrontación y conquistas**.
   The city’s survival is distinguished from the 1519 massacre; the previously
   abandoned Great Pyramid is not presented as a functioning 1519 capital.
   - <https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/node/4336>
   - <https://mexicograndezaydiversidad.inah.gob.mx/libro/fasciculos/07/>
5. José Manuel Antonio Chávez Gómez and Cuauhtémoc Velasco Ávila,
   **La Nueva España y la reconfiguración del territorio**, INAH;
   **Cimientos de un nuevo orden**. Northern Yucatán’s conquest is a prolonged
   process beginning in 1527, with Mérida in 1542 and warfare through 1546–1547.
   - <https://mexicograndezaydiversidad.inah.gob.mx/publicacion/capitulo-08.html>
   - <https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/pagina-de-elemento/4737>
6. Laura Caso Barrera, **Destino de los señores itzáes y de Cabnal...**, INAH.
   Itza submission belongs to the late seventeenth century. Nojpetén therefore
   remains independent throughout this module; its boundary is a local core.
   - <https://revistas.inah.gob.mx/index.php/antropologia/article/view/19111>

### Caribbean and Indigenous North America

7. Smithsonian NMAI, **Taíno: Native Heritage and Identity in the Caribbean**
   and **Infinity of Nations: Mesoamerica/Caribbean**, complemented by Met,
   **Central America and the Caribbean, 1400–1600**, and Library of Congress,
   **Columbus and the Taíno**. The text explicitly retains Taíno people and
   heritage after devastating conquest, disease and forced labour.
   - <https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/resources/Taino-Native-Heritage-Identity-Caribbean-Gallery-Guide>
   - <https://americanindian.si.edu/exhibitions/infinityofnations/mesoamerica-caribbean.html>
   - <https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/canc.html>
   - <https://wwws.loc.gov/exhibits/exploring-the-early-americas/columbus-and-the-taino.html>
8. Library of Congress, **Conquest and Colonialism, 1492–1808**, chapter 7;
   **Cuba in 1898**, historical background. These distinguish La Isabela in
   1494 from the start of Cuba’s conquest in 1511. Guanahani’s identification is
   disputed: the event has an approximate marker in the traditionally associated
   San Salvador area, with the uncertainty stated in all three languages.
   - <https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/21/75/83/80/2021758380/2021758380.pdf>
   - <https://guides.loc.gov/world-of-1898/cuba-overview>
   Florida Museum excavations and Kathleen Deagan’s **La Isabela: Columbus’s Outpost Among the Tainos, 1493–1498** establish the abandonment of La Isabela. Its colonial footprint ends in 1498; Santo Domingo continues.
   - <https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/histarch/research/dominican-republic/la-isabela/>
9. National Park Service, **Spanish Encounters**, Pecos;
   **Coronado’s Seven Cities, continued**; **Mission Nuestra Señora de Porciúncula**.
   These distinguish temporary expeditions in 1540–1542, Coronado’s own arrival
   in Pecos in 1541, and enduring colonisation under Oñate in 1598. The large
   seventeenth-century Pecos mission church is not introduced in 1598.
   - <https://www.nps.gov/peco/learn/historyculture/spanish-encounters.htm>
   - <https://www.nps.gov/coro/learn/historyculture/coronados-seven-cities-continued.htm>
   - <https://home.nps.gov/subjects/travelspanishmissions/mission-nuestra-senora-de-porciuncula.htm>
10. NPS, **Coosa**, and **Southeast Chronicles: Kennesaw Mountain**. Coosa is
    represented as several linked polities, with the expedition in 1540 and later
    entradas explicitly distinct from European territorial sovereignty.
    - <https://www.nps.gov/chch/learn/historyculture/coosa.htm>
    - <https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1539/the-southeast-chronicles-kennesaw-mountain-national-battlefield-park.htm>
11. University of Texas at Austin, Texas Archeological Research Laboratory,
    **Tejas: Late Caddo; Early Historic; Caddo Timeline**. Caddo society consists
    of independent groups and alliances. The 1542–1543 incursion does not create
    a colony. Its event point denotes an approximate eastern Texas region,
    not a precisely established battle site. No invented epidemic death totals
    or immediate universal collapse are used.
    - <https://www.texasbeyondhistory.net/tejas/ancestors/late.html>
    - <https://texasbeyondhistory.net/tejas/ancestors/historic.html>
    - <https://texasbeyondhistory.net/tejas/fundamentals/timeline.html>
12. Onondaga Nation, **Facts; Timeline**, and New York State Museum,
    **Haudenosaunee Longhouse**. The five original nations and common council
    are represented, but no exact founding year is asserted. Sources and
    traditions differ on chronology. Tuscarora membership is not projected back
    into the sixteenth century.
    - <https://www.onondaganation.org/aboutus/facts/>
    - <https://www.onondaganation.org/history/timeline/>
    - <https://www.nysm.nysed.gov/mohawk-haudenosaunee-village/haudenosaunee-longhouse>
13. University of Alabama, Vernon James Knight, **Ancient Site: Moundville**,
    used through the existing `lateMoundville` record. A residual settlement
    phase ends around 1500; the broader Mississippi-region communities continue.
    - <https://moundville.museums.ua.edu/ancient-site/>
14. NPS, **St. Augustine Town Plan** and **African Americans in St. Augustine**;
    **1585: The Military Colony; 1587: The Lost Colony; 1590 Rescue Voyage**.
    St. Augustine begins in 1565. Roanoke has distinct attempts and ends as a
    territorial layer before 1590; there is no lasting English mainland empire
    or anachronistic Jamestown before 1600.
    - <https://www.nps.gov/places/st-augustine-town-plan-historic-district-st-augustine-florida.htm>
    - <https://www.nps.gov/casa/learn/historyculture/african-americans-in-st-augustine-1565-1821.htm>
    - <https://home.nps.gov/fora/learn/historyculture/1585-the-military-colony.htm>
    - <https://www.nps.gov/fora/learn/historyculture/1587-the-lost-colony.htm>
    - <https://home.nps.gov/fora/learn/historyculture/1590-rescue-voyage.htm>

### Andes, northern South America and Brazil

15. Smithsonian NMAI, **The Great Inka Road: The Empire Builders**, plus the
    Inka historical timeline and geographic map. These give the order of
    Pachacuti, Tupac and Huayna Capac and the changing scale of Tawantinsuyu.
    The 1470, 1480 and 1500 silhouettes are approximate staged cores; no precise
    annual conquest boundary is asserted. The final broad domain is never
    projected back to 1454.
    - <https://americanindian.si.edu/inkaroad/inkauniverse/cusco/empire-builders.html>
    - <https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/inka-water/timeline/timeline>
    - <https://americanindian.si.edu/nk360/inka-water/pdf/SQ1-History-Timeline-Inka-Map.pdf>
16. Met, **Central and Southern Andes, 1400–1600** and **Northern Andes,
    1400–1600**. These support Chimú’s approximate 1470 conquest, late-fifteenth-
    century coastal incorporation, the 1530s wars, the viceroyalty in 1542 and
    Toledo’s late-sixteenth-century restructuring. Chincha’s incorporation is
    shown as an approximate late-fifteenth-century phase, not a securely dated
    single battle.
    - <https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/sanc.html>
    - <https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/08/sann.html>
17. Museo de Sitio de Pachacamac, **Bienvenidos al Santuario; Taurichumpi;
    Pachacamac: una historia milenaria**. These distinguish Ichma, Inca control
    beginning around 1470, and the looting of 1533. After that date the polygon
    represents inhabited Lurín communities rather than a functioning oracle.
    - <https://pachacamac.cultura.pe/bienvenidos-al-santuario-arqueologico-de-pachacamac>
    - <https://pachacamac.cultura.pe/node/171>
18. Edmundo Guillén Guillén, **Titu Cusi Yupanqui y su tiempo**, and Alice Kate
    Merry, **Between adoption and refusal: European objects at Vilcabamba,
    1537–1572**, PUCP. Independent Vilcabamba survives from 1537 to 1572. Its
    locality is excluded from Spanish Peru’s pre-1572 outline and included
    only at conquest. The post-1533 map does not leave all Tawantinsuyu intact.
    - <https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/historiaycultura/article/download/291/331>
    - <https://alicia.concytec.gob.pe/vufind/Record/PUCP_36eb353f89b7e97d403f03aa89ea9c24/Details>
19. Banco de la República, **Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada; Tisquesusa**. The
    invasion begins in 1537; the 1538 event concerns the proclamation of Spanish
    possession. This wording avoids overstating the contested details of
    Bogotá’s first urban foundation and surviving Muisca resistance.
    - <https://enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org/Gonzalo_Jim%C3%A9nez_de_Quesada>
    - <https://enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org/Tisquesusa>
20. Biblioteca Nacional de Chile, **La Guerra de Arauco: Ejército español**.
    The 1598 Curalaba victory triggers a major uprising and the beginning of the
    colonial urban network’s retreat. The text does not claim that every southern
    Spanish town disappeared within that same year.
    - <https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-92258.html>
21. Arquivo Nacional do Brasil, **Período Colonial (1500 a 1599); Governo-geral;
    Salvador; Franceses na Colônia**. Further date checks: **Câmaras Municipais**
    (São Vicente 1532) and **Duarte Coelho Pereira** (Pernambuco 1535).
    - <https://mapa.an.gov.br/index.php/assuntos/79-producao/80-cronologia/500-periodo-colonial-1500-a-1599>
    - <https://historialuso.an.gov.br/glossario/index.php/verbetes-glossario/43-verbetes-iniciados-em-g/916-governo-geral>
    - <https://historialuso.an.gov.br/glossario/index.php/verbetes-glossario/35-verbetes-iniciados-em-s/408-salvador>
    - <https://mapa.an.gov.br/index.php/assuntos/15-dicionario/57-dicionario-da-administracao-publica-brasileira-do-periodo-colonial/141-camaras-municipais>
    - <https://mapa.arquivonacional.gov.br/index.php/mapa/centrais-de-conteudo/producao/biografias/409-duarte-coelho-pereira>
22. Biblioteca Nacional do Brasil, **França Antártica**, and MultiRio,
    **A França Antártica**. The fort exists from 1555 to its destruction in 1560;
    French groups and Indigenous allies remain until 1567. These stages are
    separate. Colonial geography covers São Vicente, Pernambuco, Salvador and
    Guanabara cores, never the royal grant lines across an unoccupied continent.
    - <https://bndigital.bn.gov.br/dossies/dossie-antigo/logicas-coloniais/ancoragens/franca-antartica/>
    - <https://multirio.rio.rj.gov.br/index.php/historia-do-brasil/america-portuguesa/8730>
23. Heckenberger et al., **Pre-Columbian Urbanism, Anthropogenic Landscapes,
    and the Future of the Amazon**, Science 321 (2008), and the University of
    Florida research summary. Upper Xingu’s inhabited settlement landscape
    continues through this interval; it is not filled as Portuguese territory.
    - <https://www.loisellelab.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/Heckenberger-et-al-2008-Science.pdf>
    - <https://research.ufl.edu/publications/explore/past/fall2008/extracts/extracts01.html>

## Map implementation checks

An isolated in-memory load with the shared helper was checked before integration:

- Node syntax check passed.
- All new phase titles, descriptions and periods are three-language arrays;
  all source IDs resolve and every new phase has a corresponding area.
- Every year 1454–1600 has at most one active frame per profile, and active
  frames have active phases. All coordinates/polygons are finite and in range.
- Pre-1454 phases are deeply equal to the input catalog.
- 15 point-in-polygon checks passed: Mexico City excluded from Spanish control
  in 1520 and included in 1521; Cusco excluded in 1532 and included in 1533;
  Vilcabamba excluded from Spanish Peru through 1571 and included in 1572;
  independent Vilcabamba ends in 1572; no Portuguese Salvador in 1500/1532;
  São Vicente present in 1532, Salvador in 1549; central Amazonia excluded in
  1600; no Spanish Baracoa in 1500, present in 1511.
- Phase coordinates prevent moving colonial markers to future capitals:
  La Isabela precedes Santo Domingo, Cusco precedes Lima, São Vicente and
  Pernambuco precede Salvador. After Fort Coligny’s destruction the marker moves
  to the bay’s mainland vicinity.
- A subsequent review found that the broad post-1542 Peru polygon missed Cusco.
  An explicit Cusco core now remains in the Spanish layer from 1533 onward.
  An expanded sweep passed 1,020 assertions across every year 1454–1600:
  Mexico/Cusco/Vilcabamba/Salvador sovereignty changes, Amazonian exclusion,
  independent Tlaxcala and Purépecha capitals before 1521, and early capital
  marker locations.

The polygons are educational schematics rather than a GIS reconstruction of
every tributary enclave. The Mexica outline is separated into cores and coastal
districts so it does not swallow independent Tlaxcala or Purépecha centres.
Continuing cultural/settlement footprints marked `previous` retain geographic
regions, not unchanging political sovereignty. Settlement loss at Moundville,
imperial collapse, and colonial subjugation are explicitly separated from the
continued history of Indigenous peoples.
