# Europe and Central Asia review — 1.27.0

27 October 2026. This is a regional historical correction, not certification of every atlas boundary.

## Corrections

- The western/eastern realm reconstructions from the mid-tenth century now share a frontier through the Scheldt, Meuse, Saône and Rhône regions. Tournai, Reims and Dijon remain west; Cambrai, Metz and Strasbourg remain east. The source's broad French polygon no longer paints southern England across the Channel.
- A separate Burgundian kingdom is shown in the 933–1031 regional snapshots. Its county, Savoy, western Alpine and Provençal domains are distinguished from the French Duchy of Burgundy. The imperial inheritance is dated to 1032; the text identifies the subsequent succession struggle rather than presenting instant uncontested control.
- The reviewed 1299 outlines continue into 1300. The Rhône-side changes at Lyon (1312) and in the Dauphiné (1349) use complementary shapes. Later Hundred Years' War frames retain their other fronts and occupations.
- The Jochid/Chagatai frontier in Semirechye is aligned with the Balkhash–Tarbagatai regional belt. The same northern boundary constrains the Moghulistan successor outline from 1347. These are corrections to overlapping reconstructions, not invented annexations in 1347.

## Limits

The paths are regional reconstructions anchored to settlements, rivers and the scholarly descriptions above. They are not digitized cadastral surveys. Dughlat's sixteenth-century geographical account is corroborating regional evidence; it is not treated as an exact boundary survey of 1347. This review does not reconstruct every Monghul campaign or local ruler. The 843/870/880 Frankish partitions, the full imperial eastern/northern frontiers, and later early-modern frontiers remain separate review work. The 933 snapshot is a regional reconstruction date, not a claim that its entire frontier was created in that year.
