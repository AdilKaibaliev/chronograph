# Africa, 1601–1789: editorial and cartographic record

Local extension by `scripts/world/to1789-africa.cjs`. All intervals are half-open; 1790 means the phase includes 1789. Source URLs are editorial metadata; the public bibliography displays titles. Consulted 24 September 2026. This is a set of dated educational regional outlines, not reconstructed cadastral boundaries.

## Coverage and decisions

All 34 African profiles active in 1600 were individually continued or concluded. Added 26 profiles, giving 60 reviewed profiles, 146 new phases and outlines, and 30 located events. Earlier entries, active phases, names, coordinates and geometry are preserved through 1600.

- Morocco: Saadi succession fragmentation after 1603; the Marrakesh line ends in 1659. A small Tafilalt Alawite core precedes the capture of Fez in 1666 and Marrakesh in 1669. Different scholarly chronologies place the last transition in 1668–1669; TDV *Merakeş* supports 1669, which is used as a schematic annual threshold. Meknes is associated with Ismail from 1672, not the beginning of the dynasty. The succession crisis of 1727 and recovery under Muhammad III from 1757 receive separate phases.
- Ottoman regional government: Egypt/al-Sham, Algiers, Tunis and Tripoli keep the central Ottoman colour `#bc815a`. This does not describe all local government as direct central administration. Algiers distinguishes pashas, aghas and deys; Tunis distinguishes Muradid and Husaynid rule; Tripoli receives a Qaramanli phase in 1711. Morocco remains separate. Ceuta changes allegiance in 1640; Portugal’s treaty recognition follows in 1668.
- Sahel: Mali’s imperial network is not carried forward unchanged. Manden becomes a smaller region of successor authorities and then a cultural landscape. Dendi continues as a local Songhay network. Arma-period Timbuktu and Gao remain urban centres, not a revived unified Songhay empire. Ségou has a Biton-era formation phase and a Diarra phase from approximately 1766. No nineteenth-century conquest of Timbuktu is moved backward. Bornu remains Sayfawa-centred around Ngazargamu; the nineteenth-century Sokoto sequence is excluded. Bulala movement toward Fitri changes Kanem’s displayed centre and type.
- Senegambia and Guinea: separate Cayor, inland Jolof, Denanke/Futa Toro and Futa Jallon profiles prevent a single undifferentiated West African state. Futa Jallon’s early eighteenth-century chronology is approximate; 1727 is an editorial annual anchor from TDV. Futa Toro changes in 1776. Oyo’s tributary influence does not erase Dahomey’s court. Oyo receives a separate 1700–1729 southward-expansion phase before the tributary phase. Oyo demands after 1730 and the consolidation of payment by the mid-century are distinguished in the description; Allada changes in 1724, Ouidah in 1727. Asante begins in 1701 after victory over Denkyira. Ife is a sacred cultural centre, not a perpetually expanding empire.
- Central Africa: Kongo is not erased after Mbwila in 1665. Rival courts replace the broad old outline; Pedro IV’s return to Mbanza Kongo in 1709 restores a smaller influence zone. Ndongo and Matamba are separate: Njinga takes the Ndongo title in 1624 and Matamba becomes her base in 1631. Pungo Andongo ends as a royal centre in 1671; Matamba continues. Luanda’s Dutch interval is 1641–1648. Benguela’s port-specific interval is **1645–1648**, following the Gulbenkian/HPIP history rather than projecting Luanda’s dates across all Angola. No Dutch or Portuguese colour covers the whole modern country. Kuba, Luba and Lunda have local political/influence cores; approximate dates in oral royal chronologies remain approximate.
- Horn: Fasilides’s reign begins in 1632, but Gondar becomes the permanent capital in **1636**. Phase coordinates move to Gondar then. The 1769 Era of the Princes changes the layer to separated influence cores while retaining imperial continuity. Awsa is separated geographically from independent Harar from 1647. Oromo communities are a cultural layer, not one state. Lalibela remains a living religious centre, with its own small settlement outline.
- Swahili coast: Gedi ends as a living urban profile around the middle of the seventeenth century; it does not survive as an active state indefinitely. Fort Jesus changes in 1698, briefly to Portugal again in 1728, and back in 1729. Mazrui autonomy receives an approximate 1740s phase, anchored at 1741. Zanzibar keeps local rulers under changing maritime suzerainty. The Omani-linked layers share `#8aa6a0`, coordinated with the Arabia module. This is not a nineteenth-century Zanzibar territorial empire. Mogadishu and Kilwa keep local profiles; Omani maritime connections do not colour all Somalia or Tanzania. Mozambique remains a small Portuguese island/port layer; its 1761 vila status, verified by HPIP, supplies the later administrative phase.
- Zimbabwe plateau: Mutapa continues through Portuguese intervention and the rise of the Rozvi, with a reduced northeastern outline. It does not vanish in 1629 or 1693. Khami/Torwa shifts toward Danangombe in the later seventeenth century; 1650 is a broad archaeological/urban transition, not a precise day of abandonment. The Rozvi threshold uses 1684 as an approximate anchor for Dombo’s attested military ascendancy; 1693 changes the regional influence outline. Great Zimbabwe is an inherited cultural landscape rather than a still-functioning imperial capital.
- Madagascar/Comoros: separate coastal communities, Imerina and Sakalava cores; no premature single island-wide monarchy. Menabe and Boina are two Sakalava centres. Ambohimanga’s outer gates are dated to 1787 by the ICOMOS evaluation; no conquest of all Madagascar is attached to that year.
- Southern Africa: Table Bay begins with a small VOC settlement in 1652, expands around Stellenbosch after 1679 and has a later uneven frontier influence zone. The 1779–1781 war is located near the Fish River frontier; a colonial claim is not rendered as comprehensive direct rule. Khoekhoe/San communities continue after settlement, disease and dispossession. Xhosa political formations remain present.
